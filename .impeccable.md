# iSchool — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users

**Primary: the parent (the buyer).** This is the *post-free-session* page — it loads right after a child finishes a free trial coding session. The parent is deciding, often within minutes of seeing their child light up, whether to pay for ongoing 1-on-1 lessons. They are evaluating legitimacy, outcomes, and value-for-money. Many are on mobile, mid-distraction, and price-sensitive (Egypt market, EGP pricing).

**Secondary: the child (ages 6–18, grades 1–12).** Often in the room, freshly excited from the session. The page should reward that excitement — the learning journey and career outcomes should feel real and aspirational — without ever talking down to the parent.

**The job to be done:** convince a parent, in one scroll, that enrolling their child is (1) credible, (2) worth the money, and (3) an exciting, concrete path — then make enrolling effortless. The page must serve conversion clarity, confidence-building, *and* journey-showcasing at once.

**Context of use:** post-trial, high-intent but skeptical, frequently mobile, bilingual (English + Arabic with full RTL).

### Brand Personality

- **Three words:** *credible, warm, aspirational.*
- **Voice:** a trusted educator, not a SaaS funnel. Confident and specific (real syllabi, real outcomes, real prices) rather than hype-y. Speaks to parents as partners in their child's growth.
- **Emotional goals:** the parent should feel *reassured* (this is legit, my money is safe), *proud* (my child could really become this), and *clear* (I know exactly what I'm buying and what happens next). Pride and optimism over urgency and pressure.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Trustworthy & premium.** Refined neutrals carry the page; one strong brand color anchors it; the warm accents are rare and earned.

- **Theme:** Light. This is a daytime, high-trust purchasing decision for parents — light mode reads as open, honest, and premium. (Dark would read "cool/techy" and undercut parental trust.)
- **Color:** Keep the iSchool brand colors — blue `#056FEC` / deep `#043FAD`, yellow `#FFD700`, orange `#FF7F1C` — but rebalance toward 60-30-10. Blue is the anchor; yellow and orange become *sharp, sparing accents* (CTAs, "recommended," key numbers), not page-wide fills. Migrate to OKLCH, tint neutrals subtly toward the brand blue, and reduce chroma near white/black. No gradient text. No cyan-on-dark AI palette.
- **Typography:** Move beyond Nunito's all-rounded, kid-flavored uniformity. Pair a **distinctive, confident display face** (for headlines, prices, the value proposition) with a **clean, highly legible body face** that also has strong Arabic support. Use a real modular scale with high contrast between steps — let typography, not emoji, carry the hierarchy. Reserve emoji/icons for genuine wayfinding, not decoration above every heading.
- **Layout:** Generous whitespace, varied spacing rhythm, intentional asymmetry. Avoid endless identical card grids and the "hero metric" template. Let the learning path and packages breathe. Body text capped at ~65–75ch.
- **Anti-references:** generic dashboard/admin look (side-stripe cards, evenly-distributed timid palettes), loud "kids' app" maximalism, pushy infomercial urgency, and anything that reads as obviously AI-generated.

### Design Principles

1. **Earn trust before asking for money.** Every section should answer a parent's silent "is this legit, and is it worth it?" — credible specifics over hype, proof over promises.
2. **One strong color, rare warm accents.** Blue anchors; yellow/orange punctuate. Accents work because they're scarce — overuse kills their power.
3. **Premium = restraint + typography.** Hierarchy comes from type scale, weight, and whitespace — not from emoji, borders, or color everywhere. Less, but more confident.
4. **Make the journey concrete and the next step effortless.** The learning path and outcomes should feel tangible and exciting; the enroll decision should be unmistakable and low-friction.
5. **Bilingual and accessible by construction.** Full EN/AR with proper RTL, and WCAG AA contrast, focus states, and reduced-motion support are non-negotiable foundations — not afterthoughts.
